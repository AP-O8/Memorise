      How this works
      • Type it — type every word out. Capitals and punctuation are filled in for you, and typing them anyway is harmless. A word appears once you get it right; press space to submit a word that has too few or too many letters. Autocorrect accepts a near-miss and shows it in yellow; switch it off for strict spelling.
      • First letter — type only the first letter of the next word and the rest of it is written for you. The letter you supplied stays highlighted. Quick drilling of the order of an essay.
      • Multiple choice — pick the next word from four options (click, or press 1–4). The wrong ones are other words from the same paragraph, chosen for a similar length. Easiest mode: use it on a first pass.
      • Switching mode starts the card again, and the same peek rules apply in all three.
      • Synonyms and skipped fillers (Type it mode) — typing show where the essay says illustrates is accepted, and the real word is written in blue so you still see the wording. Skipping a filler word or two (that, the, of) is allowed if the next word you type is right; the skipped word appears greyed. Typing an extra filler the essay does not have is ignored rather than marked wrong. Switch it off with Accept synonyms & skipped fillers for word-perfect practice.
      • Study text works only before your first keystroke. Once you start typing, the full text stays hidden until the run ends.
      • One peek per run. Peek the word you are on, or click any word. It stays highlighted so you can see what needs work — and a second peek sends you back to the beginning.
      • Read only — no typing at all: the paragraph is laid out as a first-letters prompt sheet (M_____ p_______ S____ a_ a c_____ s______.) for reciting from, out loud or in your head. Punctuation stays visible. Click any word to uncover it and click again to cover it back up; Study text shows the lot.
      • Sentence ticks — each sentence has a numbered box in front of it. Tick the ones you already know and they are written in and stepped over, so you start at the first sentence you have not learnt. Ticks are kept with the card, in every mode, and Untick all brings the whole thing back.
      • Esc or Cmd/Ctrl+R restarts. Highlights survive the restart; the text does not.
